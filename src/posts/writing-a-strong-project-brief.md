# Writing a Project Brief That Gets You a Better Quote

The quality of a freelance quote is a direct reflection of the quality of the brief behind it. A vague request produces a vague estimate. Here is what I ask every client to define before we talk numbers.

## Describe the outcome, not just the feature

"I need a booking system" tells me what to build but not why. "I need clients to book a call without emailing back and forth" tells me what problem we're actually solving — and often changes the recommended solution.

## Share real examples

Two or three reference sites — even ones you only partially like — communicate more about tone, density, and interaction style than a paragraph of adjectives ever could.

## Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves

Every project has a core that must work on day one, and a wishlist that can follow in a second phase. Naming that split upfront keeps scope, budget, and timeline realistic.

## Bring your constraints into the open

Launch dates, existing brand guidelines, hosting requirements, and budget ranges are not awkward details — they are the fastest way to get an accurate proposal instead of a generic one.

> A strong brief is not extra work before the project starts. It is the first deliverable of the project.
